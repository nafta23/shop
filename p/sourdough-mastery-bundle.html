You do not need a temperature-controlled fermentation chamber or a bakery-proof cabinet to bake sourdough at home. A few basic items are enough to get started. The flour you choose dictates how your starter behaves. For a reliable, everyday routine, stick to these two options. 1. Whole Rye or Whole Wheat (for the initial jump-start) These flours contain more wild yeast and nutrients than white flours. They kick-start fermentation quickly. Use this flour for the first 3–5 feedings to wake a dormant starter or establish a new one. 2. Bread Flour (for maintenance) Once your starter is active, switch to a strong white bread flour (high protein). This provides the stable environment needed for a reliable levain. Avoid "all-purpose" flours with very low protein content if you want good rise and structure. Practical example: Buy a 1kg bag of bread flour and a small pack of whole rye. Use the rye for the first week, then transition to the bread flour for daily feeding. Consistency beats duration. A short, daily feed keeps your starter healthy without consuming your day. Key timing: If you bake weekly, you can store your starter in the fridge and feed it once a week using the same 1:1:2 ratio (starter:flour:water) before using it in a recipe.
