Most productivity advice is built for neurotypical brains. For the ADHD brain, the standard "to-do list" often fails because it relies on sustained attention—a resource that feels scarce. Instead of fighting your brain's natural wiring, this planner works with it. The goal for Day 1 is not to change who you are, but to map your current patterns so you can build structure around them. Attention with ADHD is often "interest-based." You can hyper-focus on tasks that are novel, urgent, or personally rewarding, but struggle with tasks that feel mundane or distant. This isn't a character flaw; it is a neurological trait. By observing when your energy spikes and dips over the next few days, you’ll start to see your personal rhythms. Take note of these three patterns without judgment: Your environment acts as external scaffolding for your brain. For ADHD minds, a cluttered space often equals a cluttered mind, increasing the number of "visual hooks" that pull your attention away from the task at hand. The "Launch Pad" Strategy: Designate one specific spot (a table, a desk corner) as your "Launch Pad." This is where your current sprint task, a notebook, and a pen live. When you aren't working, everything else is put away. When you sit at the Launch Pad, your brain receives a clear signal: "It is time to focus." Keep this area minimal. If you need tools, use drawer organizers or trays so items have a home and aren't scattered. Sensory Regulation: Pay attention to your senses. Digital Hygiene: Start simple.
