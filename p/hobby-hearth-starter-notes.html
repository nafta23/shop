The goal is to keep your neck and shoulders relaxed while you work at a 1:1 scale. A standard dining table (28–30 inches) is often too high for detailed brush work. Aim for a surface height of 24–26 inches if you will be seated, or 34–36 inches if you prefer standing. If your desk is too high, place a cushion or a few folded blankets on your chair seat to lower your eyeline. If it is too low, raise the desk on sturdy blocks or use a sturdy folding table balanced on risers. Your elbows should form roughly a 90-degree angle when your hands rest on the surface, and your feet should rest flat on the floor or a footrest. Avoid overhead fluorescent lights, which cast shadows down onto the miniature and create glare on the paint. Instead, use a focused, adjustable light source. Position a gooseneck LED lamp or a daylight-balanced desk lamp (5000K–6500K) to the side of your dominant hand. The light should hit the miniature from an angle of about 45 degrees. This creates subtle shadows that help you see surface details and avoid "painting in the dark" on recessed areas. If you are right-handed, place the light to your left; left-handed painters should mirror this setup. Avoid placing the light directly in front of or behind you, as both cause eye strain and obscure details.
