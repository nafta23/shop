Lighting is the single most impactful factor in miniature painting. Natural daylight is ideal, but inconsistent. A dedicated light source allows for consistent color matching and detail work regardless of the time of day. Avoid relying on a single overhead lamp, which creates harsh shadows and glare. Instead, use a daylight-balanced lamp (5000K to 6500K) positioned to illuminate the work surface from the side or front. Many hobbyists use clamp-on LED lamps with adjustable arms, allowing the light to be directed precisely where needed without casting shadows from the painter's own hand. For those working in shared spaces, a lightbox or a bright white backdrop can help separate the miniature from the surrounding environment, making colors pop and details easier to discern. The goal is to mimic natural north-light conditions, which provide even, diffused illumination without the yellow cast of tungsten bulbs or the blue cast of some cool white LEDs. Painting miniatures often involves hours of fine motor work. An ergonomic setup prevents fatigue and improves precision. The miniature should be positioned at eye level or slightly below. A rotating painting handle or a lazy Susan allows the artist to access all sides of the model without twisting the wrist or leaning over the work surface. This constant adjustment reduces strain and ensures that highlights and shadows are applied consistently around the model. The work surface itself should be stable and at a comfortable height. Many painters use a non-slip mat or a dedicated painting surface with a slight texture to prevent the miniature from sliding during detail work. …
